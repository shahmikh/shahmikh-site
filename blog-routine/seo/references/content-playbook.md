# Content playbook: pages that rank and get cited

## Before writing
- **One primary search phrase** per page (the words buyers type), plus 5–8 sub-questions they ask next. Use Search Console queries, "People also ask", autocomplete, and what AI answers cite today.
- **Search intent decides the format**: "how much does X cost" → cost breakdown with ranges and factors; "X vs Y" → fair comparison table and recommendation; "how to" → numbered steps; "best X for Y" → criteria first, honest picks (never rank yourself #1).
- **Non-commodity angle** (required): a real example, screenshot, workflow, tested result, original data, or a clear opinion with the reason. If there is none, the page probably shouldn't exist yet.
- Check it doesn't duplicate an existing page (cannibalization). Update the existing page instead when the topic overlaps.

## Page structure (answer-first)
1. **Title** ≤60 characters, phrase near the start; add the year only when time-sensitive.
2. **H1** close to the title, one per page.
3. **Lead**: answer the search in the first 2 sentences.
4. **Key takeaways box**: 3–5 one-sentence answers that make sense on their own (AI engines lift these).
5. **Table of contents** for long pages.
6. **H2s phrased as real questions** ("How much does…", "Why does…"); the first 1–2 sentences under each are a direct, self-contained answer, then the detail.
7. Lists, numbered steps and small tables where they make an answer clearer; define terms in plain English the first time.
8. **One practical checklist or plan** the reader can use today.
9. **FAQ**: 3 real questions, 2–3 sentences each (mark up as FAQPage for machine readability; no rich results since May 2026).
10. **Sources box**: 2–4 authoritative primary sources, also linked inline where the claim is made, mirrored in JSON-LD `citation`.
11. **Author box**: photo, real credentials, links to About and LinkedIn, editorial policy link.
12. **Related links** to the money pages and related posts, then a clear call to action.

Length follows the question: usually 1,200–2,000 words for guides, shorter for narrow answers. Never pad.

## Writing quality rules
- Specific over vague: name the actual setting, field, tool or step.
- No invented statistics, studies, client names, results or quotes. Hypothetical numbers must be labelled as examples.
- Vary sentence rhythm; don't start consecutive paragraphs the same way; avoid triplets of adjectives.
- Avoid filler that reads as machine-written: "in today's fast-paced world", "in the digital age", "unlock", "unleash", "elevate", "game-changer", "dive in", "navigate the landscape", "it's important to note", "in conclusion", "whether you're X or Y", "look no further", "seamless", "robust", "leverage" (verb), "supercharge". Prefer commas/periods over em dashes.
- Match the audience's spelling and units (US: inquiries, ZIP code, dollars).
- First person for real practice ("When I set this up…"), never for invented client stories.
- Compliance topics (health, legal, finance, texting): accurate caveats, link the regulator, say it isn't legal/medical/tax advice.

## Money pages (services, pricing, industries)
- Answer the buyer's sub-questions on the page: what's included, process and timeline, price ranges or what drives price, who it's for and not for, proof (case studies with outcomes, reviews), FAQ.
- Pricing pages and honest comparison pages get cited for "cost" and "vs" queries; ranges are fine if real.
- Case studies: one URL each when possible, with the client (or anonymized descriptor), problem, solution, timeline, and real outcome numbers. Without outcomes they are process examples, label them honestly.

## Proof and trust
- Testimonials: real name, role, company (or permission-based anonymity), photo, link to the original review where possible. Remove anything unverifiable.
- Claims like "1,500+ projects" need something behind them (portfolio, platform profile).
- About page: background, credentials with links, tools, how content is made (disclose AI assistance honestly), contact details.

## Freshness and refreshes
- Monthly: find pages ranking positions 8–30 or with many impressions and few clicks in Search Console; improve the answer, add an example/FAQ/source, sharpen title and description.
- Update `dateModified`, the visible "Updated" date and sitemap `lastmod` together, only for real content changes.

## Publishing pace
- Quality over volume. For a small site, ~2 strong posts a week is plenty; mass-producing pages risks the scaled-content policy and core-update losses. Every post must pass the non-commodity test.

## Internal linking
- Every new page links to its service page, industry/topic hub and 1–2 related posts with descriptive anchors (never "click here"); an older relevant page links back to it within days.

## Distribution (feeds off-site signals)
- Share each post on LinkedIn with a short native summary; make a short video for top posts and embed it (VideoObject). Answer related questions where the audience is (Reddit, communities) without spamming links.
