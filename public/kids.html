What your child gets each week

The 8-week plan

The weeks follow the five big ideas of the AI4K12 guidelines for school-age AI education: perception, representation and reasoning, learning, natural interaction and societal impact. Tools and project difficulty are adjusted to each child's age and level.

Week 1: What is AI, and how does a computer "see"?

Perception. Kids see how machines sense the world, then build a simple "guess the picture" activity. Project: a first AI experiment they can demo.

Week 2: How machines learn from examples

Learning. Kids train a simple classifier with their own examples and watch it get things right and wrong. Project: train and test a small model.

Week 3: Data, fairness and mistakes

Societal impact. Why does AI get things wrong, and who decides what is fair? Kids audit a small dataset for gaps. Project: improve a model by fixing its data.

Week 4: Rules, decisions and thinking in steps

Representation and reasoning. Decision trees and rule-based thinking, the logic underneath much of computer science. Project: a quiz or helper bot that makes decisions.

Week 5: Talking to AI: how chatbots work

Natural interaction. How language models work at a child's level, and how to write clear instructions. Done live with the teacher present. Project: a character chatbot with safety rules.

Week 6: Checking AI's answers

Societal impact and critical thinking. AI can sound confident and be wrong. Kids learn to check sources and spot made-up answers. Project: a fact-check report.

Week 7: Build week

Kids choose their own project, such as a game, helper or small AI demo, and build it with guidance in both live sessions.

Week 8: Showcase

Each child presents their project to the group, and parents are invited to join. Every child receives a certificate of completion and a plan for what to learn next.

Questions parents ask

Does my child need coding experience?

No. We start from the basics, and each week's project is scaled to the child's level.

What ages is it for?

The first cohort is for ages 10 to 14. Younger children need a different format, so they are not part of this first cohort.

How is this different from coding apps?

Every class is live with a real teacher who can spot misunderstandings as they happen, and every week ends with a project your child presents.

What does it cost?

Founding-cohort pricing is shared with families on the waitlist before any seats open. Joining the waitlist is free and you are under no obligation.

Are sessions recorded?

Recording is available on request so children can review material afterward. Ask before the session starts.
