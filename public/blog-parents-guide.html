Guiding Your Teen Into a Tech Career: A Parent's Guide

Most parents I work with aren't trying to become technical experts themselves — they just want to know they're helping their teenager make good decisions. Here's what I'd actually focus on.

What matters right now (AP/IB stage)

  • Consistency over intensity. Regular, structured practice in AP CSP, AP CSA or IB CS beats occasional cramming — the subject builds cumulatively. Not sure which AP course to pick? Here is how AP CSP and AP CSA compare.
  • Understanding over memorizing. A student who can explain why code works will outperform one who's just memorized syntax, especially in IB's Internal Assessment.
  • Curiosity signals. If your teen is naturally drawn to how things work — not just gaming or apps, but systems — that's a genuine signal worth nurturing, not just a school requirement to get through.

What you can safely ignore for now

  • Which specific programming language is "best" — the fundamentals transfer between languages far more than people assume.
  • Whether they need a certification immediately — certifications become valuable later, once fundamentals are solid; front-loading them too early usually backfires.
  • Comparing them to other students online — tech learning paths are genuinely non-linear, and comparison mostly just adds pressure.

The one thing worth doing early

Expose them to the breadth of what "tech" actually includes — it's not just software development. Cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, networking, and AI operations are all real, well-paying paths that get far less classroom attention than coding does. A student who discovers early that they love networking or security, rather than assuming "tech" only means writing apps, ends up on a much better-fitted path.

If you want a second opinion on where your teen actually stands and what to prioritize next, that's exactly what a discovery call is for — no pressure, just an honest read.
